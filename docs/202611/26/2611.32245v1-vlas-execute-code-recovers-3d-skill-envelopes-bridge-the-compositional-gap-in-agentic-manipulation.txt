Abstract —Composing frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) skills under an agentic planner promises scalable long-horizon manipulation without long-horizon demonstrations, yet strong atomic skills routinely fail at the seams between them even under a correct plan. Standard remedies either reset the robot’s pose or draft entry conditions in language, missing the physical cause we uncover through controlled causal interventions: even after re-homing, a frozen VLA rigidly retraces its habitual trajectory and collides with displaced scene residue, whether or not it can see it. To address this, we propose the Skill Envelope , a policy-measured 3D geometric precondition that captures the volume a frozen skill sweeps during its own successful executions.

Grounded in the insight that the very rigidity making a frozen skill fragile at seams also makes its trajectory predictable in advance, we expose each envelope to an agent as two classical 3D tools (C HECK to detect blocking residue before invocation and FREE to locate safe placement regions), establishing a division of labor where VLAs execute in-distribution manipulation and code repairs out-of-distribution world states. On LIBERO-Goal with frozen π0.5, our zero-failure envelope predicts collisions at 0.946 AUROC (0.944 from one RGB-D view) and beats frontier VLMs on seam readiness (0.902 vs. 0.793); using it to guide skill ordering and code repair lifts success from 82.8% to 92.3% on two-step seams and from 29.7% to 93.8% on drawer-bearing 3– 4 step chains, matching privileged world reset. Together, these results show that reliable VLA composition hinges on measuring a policy’s physical workspace rather than guessing preconditions in language, offering a training-free geometric interface between high-level planners and frozen visuomotor skills. To test whether this interface holds beyond a fixed simulated kitchen and deter-ministic plans, Stage 2 will close the loop with autonomous LLM orchestration and synthesized free-object recovery, and evaluate arXiv:2611.32245v1 [cs.RO] 24 Nov 2026 cross-policy transfer on cluttered benchmarks and real hardware with online RGB-D sensing. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Everyday long-horizon manipulation cannot be solved by enumerating end-to-end demonstrations: even ten atomic skills yield 5,040 four-step permutations, each creating distinct intermediate world states. A natural alternative is agentic composition : pairing a high-level planner or LLM agent with a library of frozen manipulation or Vision-Language-Action (VLA) skills [1, 4, 13, 16]. Yet strong atomic skills routinely collapse at the transitions (or seams ) between them. Consider a two-step kitchen routine in LIBERO-Goal [5]: open the middle drawer, then push the plate to the front of the stove. A frozen π0.5 policy [9] executes both skills reliably in isolation. Chained directly, however, the second skill drops to 0/10; even after returning the robot arm to its home pose ( re-homing ), the standard fix, it remains at 0/10 because the arm runs into the open drawer (Fig. 1). Across 64 ordered skill pairs (640 handoff states), mean success drops from 98.0% in isolation to 14.2% under direct chaining, and recovers only to 82.8% after re-homing. The predecessor is 

done , yet the successor is not ready .Recent work has recognized this compositional gap, but ad-dresses it through proxies rather than querying the policy itself. Skill-chaining frameworks address only the robot’s configura-tion while leaving scene objects untouched [15, 12, 7]. Agen-tic orchestrators prompt a language model to draft natural-language entry conditions at seams and invoke programmatic primitives when those conditions fail [13], or ask a vision-language model whether to advance or retry [10]. Others diagnose scene-residue failures only after the policy gets stuck, relying on learned recovery policies or fine-tuning [11]. What remains missing is a physical question: what exact precondition does a frozen policy require to succeed, and how can an agent check it beforehand? 

We answer this question in two steps. First, by intervening on the exact same 640 re-homed handoff states, we causally separate visual observation shift from physical world shift. Contrary to the common assumption that seam failures stem primarily from visual distractors, rendering residual objects invisible in the camera feed barely helps, while physically removing them recovers most of what re-homing leaves and slashes collisions (Fig. 2a). Seam failure is therefore governed by physical, policy-specific hidden preconditions : a frozen VLA follows its habitual trajectory and collides with any residue standing in its way. Second, we turn this weakness into a measurement tool: the rigidity that makes a frozen skill fragile at seams is the very property that makes its trajectory measurable in advance. By running each skill in isolation, without human demonstrations or failure rollouts, we record the 3D volume swept by the arm, gripper, and carried object, yielding a Skill Envelope . Exposed to an agent as two classical 3D tools (C HECK and F REE ), the envelope establishes a clean division of labor: VLA policies execute in-distribution manipulation, classical code repairs out-of-distribution world states, and the boundary between them is drawn by policy measurement rather than language. 

In this Stage-1 submission, we establish the empirical foundation and core mechanism of this framework: 1) Causal seam diagnosis (Sec. II): We decompose the compositional gap across body, view, and world states on the same 640 handoffs, showing that failures remaining after robot re-homing are dominated by policy-specific physical collisions rather than visual shift. 2) Skill Envelopes as agent tools (Sec. III): We in-troduce an O(n), zero-failure 3D envelope profiling method and formalize two geometric tools (C HECK 

and F REE ) linking frozen VLAs with classical recovery code, supporting both pre-built primitives and future agent-synthesized skills. 3) Validation across sensing, language, and multi-step seams (Sec. IV): We show that zero-failure envelopes detect collisions even from one RGB-D view, generalize across unseen predecessors and categories, and beat frontier VLMs. Grounding why cleanup belongs to code over the VLA, envelope-guided planning and repair match privileged reset on two-skill seams and compound on 3–4 step chains. II. W HY SKILLS FAIL AT SEAMS : A C AUSAL DIAGNOSIS 

To understand why high-performing atomic skills break when composed, we evaluate a frozen π0.5 policy on the 64 ordered two-skill pairs of LIBERO-Goal whose goals involve disjoint objects or parts (ten handoff states each, 640 in total). When predecessor A finishes and successor B starts, three factors simultaneously deviate from B’s training distribution: the robot’s body configuration, the visual appearance of the scene ( view ), and the physical presence of objects moved or articulated by A (world ). 

Physical collision dominates visual shift. We disentangle all three factors via controlled interventions on the exact same 640 handoff states, each snapshotted in full and replayed with identical policy noise (Fig. 2a). Directly chaining A → B

collapses success from 98.0% (isolated) to 14.2%. Standard 

re-homing removes body shift and recovers success to 82.8%, leaving a 15.2-point residual gap. On these identical re-homed states, we next apply View Reset : hiding residual objects from the renderer while keeping physical colliders intact. Strikingly, View Reset improves success by only +1.7 points (95% CI [ −0.5, +3.9]) and leaves arm-residue collisions virtually unchanged (17.0% vs. 17.8%). In our running example (open middle drawer → push plate to stove), erasing the drawer from the image still causes the arm to run into it in 10 of 10 handoff states. In contrast, World Reset , which physically restores residual objects via state teleportation, recovers +10.0 points (92.8%) and drops collisions to 2.0%. While visual shift can affect isolated seams or narrower policies [14], in shared-workspace multi-step tasks with a strong pretrained VLA, re-homed seam failure is dominated by physical collision.

A. Measuring the Skill Envelope 

Because a frozen skill k executes repeatable trajectories within its training distribution, we can measure its spatial requirements directly from the policy itself. We run k in isolation from its nominal start states and discard any un-successful episodes, keeping N successful rollouts (37 to 89 here, depending on the skill). At each control step, forward kinematics maps the robot’s links, gripper, and grasped object into a world-frame grid of 1 cm voxels, marking those their oriented bounding boxes pass through. Aggregating occupancy across the N executions yields the Skill Envelope , the empir-ical probability that skill k sweeps through voxel x:

D. Why Code Repairs, and Two-Step Seam Recovery (RQ4) 

Prompting frozen π0.5 with two inverse phrasings to close a drawer succeeds only 1/280 (0.4%), whereas a concise slide primitive succeeds 138/140 (98.6%) on 140 fresh drawer handoffs (Fig. 1), grounding our VLA–code split. Gating this primitive with C HECK matches privileged World Reset via real actions (Table Ib; an earlier Cartesian-homing run got 90.5% vs. 92.7%). Rather than always closing drawers, CHECK catches all 70/70 failing states and skips 20 safe ones (always-repair ties at 92.3% with 20 extra actions). On 140 fresh drawer handoffs, proactive repair matches privileged reset and beats reactive repair (Table Ib). 

E. Do Seam Gains Compound on Multi-Step Chains? (RQ5) 

We test compounding on 256 3–4 goal chains requiring all goals at termination (Fig. 3c). On 128 drawer-bearing chains, envelope-guided ordering and repair adds +64.1 pts over random order with re-homing ( p=1 .5×10 −5, 16/16 positive), matching privileged reset. VI. R ELATED WORK 

Seam diagnosis and skill chaining. Recent studies show frozen skills fail at seams [14], yet remedies either address only robot pose while leaving residue intact [15, 12, 7], or, closest to ours, Wagh et al. [11] detect stalls post-hoc and apply learned recovery and fine-tuning. Because prior resets alter physics and visuals together, they conflate visual and physical shift. We first separate body, view, and world shifts on identical handoffs, showing physical collisions dominate and preventing them pre-execution without training. 

Agentic orchestration of frozen VLAs. Agentic frameworks pair planners with frozen VLAs for contact-rich skills and analytic primitives for free space [16]. Closest, BATON [13] prompts an LLM to draft language entry conditions at seams and invokes analytic recovery when checks fail. We retain this split but replace language-guessed conditions with policy-measured 3D preconditions: an LLM reasons over task seman-tics, not a frozen policy’s swept corridor. 

Geometric preconditions of visuomotor policies. Stage-Craft [8] uses a VLM over labeled single-task rollouts to clear obstructions pre-execution. Closest geometrically, DR-LfD [2] replays human demos to extract swept volumes for self-trained policies in symbolic TAMP. Skill Envelopes instead profile the 

frozen VLA itself , with no demos, failures, or symbolic model, to resolve self-inflicted seam residue pre-execution. VII. C ONCLUSION 

We showed that seam failures persisting after re-homing in composed frozen VLAs are dominated by physical, policy-specific collisions rather than visual shift. Turning a policy’s trajectory rigidity into an O(n), zero-failure Skill Envelope lets an agent flag blocking residue pre-execution and repair out-of-distribution states with concise code. Drawing the neural–code boundary via measurement rather than language gives a clean, training-free foundation for agentic manipulation. REFERENCES
